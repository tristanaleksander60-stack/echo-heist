## Before

- **Consent:** written parental consent (a text message is fine). We film **the screen only**, never faces. Delete the videos after the notes are written.
- **Accounts:** the kid plays on **their own avatar** if at all possible. That is the whole point ("it's ME"). Use an early-tester invite to their account; if it doesn't work for under-13 accounts, let the kid restyle a spare account to look like their usual avatar first.
- **Devices:** the kid's own phone, plus one cheap Android if you have one.
- **Mix:** at least one pair of friends (co-op) and one kid who reads slowly or has another first language. In rounds 1-2, half the kids start at level 2.
- **Write the pass bars down before you start** (below). No changing them after.

## During: the rules

1. Say only: **"This is a new game. Play it like you would at home. I can't help you, but you can think out loud."**
2. **Don't help.** If they ask, say "What do you think?" Each time they ask for help, write the time on the sheet.
3. **Neutral questions only**, never leading ones. "What happened when you pressed that?" is fine; "Did you see your clone?" is not.
4. Write down every stop over 20 seconds where they don't know what to do.

## After: four questions (write the answers word for word)

1. "Tell me what this game is about."
2. "Who were the other people running around?" Pass: they say it's them, or a copy of them.
3. Show three 10-second clips (ours and two top Roblox games), then ask: "Which looks the best?"
4. "Do you want to keep playing?"

**Rounds 1-2 also:** show a 64 px picture of 4 avatars, one of them their own clone, and ask "Which one is you?"

## Pass bars (rounds 1-2)

- First clone ≤ 30 s and first bank ≤ 45 s for ≥ 4 of 5 kids.
- Each kid explains the clones in their own words after L1.
- No stall over 20 s in L1-L2.
- 4 of 5 find their own clone.
- ≥ 3 of 5 ask to keep playing.
